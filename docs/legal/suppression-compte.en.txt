Deleting your Onyx account

This English version is a translation provided for convenience. The French version is the authoritative text and prevails in case of any discrepancy.

Last updated: October 21, 2026

Before you start
Onyx is an app published by Tsuky. It is the player for a media library hosted on a server that you, or someone close to you, install and administer. Your account exists only on that server: the publisher has no account in your name and holds no data about you. Deletion therefore happens on the server you connect to.

From the app (recommended)
1. Open Onyx and sign in to the server in question.
2. Go to Settings, then My account.
3. Tap Delete my account.
4. Confirm with your password.
Deletion is immediate and permanent. If you have an account on several servers, repeat these steps on each one.

Without the app
Ask the server administrator to delete it: they can delete your account from their server's user management. If you cannot reach them, write to tsuky.4400@gmail.com with the server address and your username: the publisher has no access to servers, but will help you find out what to do.

If you own the server
The owner account cannot be deleted while it owns the server. First transfer ownership to another account, or simply delete your server: all data goes with it.

Data deleted
As soon as you confirm, the server erases your account and everything attached to it: username and password, sessions and signed-in devices, watch progress and history, hidden titles and lists, playback and track settings, shares, invitations, TV pairings and links to other services.

Data kept
Backups: the server keeps its last three automatic backups, made before each update that changes its database. They may contain your account until later updates replace them.
Services connected by the administrator: media requests sent to a request manager, or data from an Emby sync, remain in those services. Ask the administrator to have them deleted.
Your device: settings, cache and downloaded files stay on the device until the app is uninstalled.
No other copy is kept, and the publisher holds none.
